Session 1: The Town, the Score, and the Noise You Make

Goal for this session: understand what you are looking at, how the score works, and why being loud costs you. No exploits yet. By the end you should be able to start a run, open a target, read the Alert meter, and reset the town.

1.1 The map is the truth

The map is a pixel-art isometric drawing of Packet River, population 646, with a transparent live overlay on top. Everything that lights up, floods, or goes dark is driven off a state snapshot the map server broadcasts once a second. Traffic cycles green and red. A train runs the top track and loops off screen. Water and power flow to thirty-odd house markers. The river runs clean with swimmers in it.

Click any building and its real service opens in a side panel, starting with a login portal, the same convention as the Cross Creek HMIs and the Widgetorium login. When you land an exploit and submit its flag, the map degrades: a shop goes defaced or db_dumped or carded, a feeder trips and a zone goes dark, the sewage plant's outfall runs raw and a brown mosaic spreads from the swimming beach, the rail switch throws and the train runs the spur into the widget factory and it catches fire.

1.2 Flags are the only currency

There is no "award points" endpoint. The scoreboard mints a random PKTR{...} flag per technique at boot and drops each one into the single service that owns it, inside the vulnerable resource, in a spot that requires the actual exploit to reach. The only write path is POST /api/score/submit {flag}. Points are a pure server-side function of a valid random flag plus server-held timing and Alert state. No client-supplied score, multiplier, or "technique complete" field is trusted.

The formula rewards three things: speed (a decaying multiplier from arm to submit), stealth (a bonus if the Alert Level did not rise while you worked the technique), and chaining (a stacking bonus for distinct techniques solved on the same subsystem in one run). Call arm when you start recon on a technique so the clock and the stealth baseline start then.

1.3 The Alert Level, and the blue team

A leaky-bucket "heat" accumulator with a roughly two-minute half-life drives an Alert Level from 0 (calm) to 5 (lockdown). Heat comes from the inherent loudness of what you did (a payroll drain is loud however quietly you got admin), from wrong flag submissions, and from a tailer reading the gateway's access log for scan bursts and tool user-agents. Thresholds have downward hysteresis so the meter does not flap.

Each level the meter crosses, a blue team responds, and a couple of the responses actually bite:

LevelResponse
1SOC investigating (banner)
2credentials rotated on the field controllers and the HMIs: an in-progress attacker's next canned command fails
3rate limiting (banner; the segmented build wires it for real)
4OT segmentation (banner; the segmented build drops the routes)
5automatic golden-restore of the worst-hit subsystem

Already-solved techniques stay solved. But if you are mid-chain when Level 2 fires and you were relying on a default credential, you just lost it.

1.4 Timed events: the town has a clock

With PKT_EVENTS at calm or lively, three things happen on a schedule and change the stakes:

  • News crew. A TV crew parks at whatever is most broken. While they film, any Alert heat from an incident on that subsystem is multiplied. Getting caught loud on camera is worse than getting caught loud.
  • State inspector. An inspector visits for a fixed window. An incident that is still active when they leave costs a large heat spike and a citation, if the town was clean when they arrived. It rewards cleaning up your mess, or never being seen.
  • Founder's Day parade. Parade crowds fill the crossroads. A signal hijacked to all-green during the parade racks up crashes several times faster. Same exploit, far bigger consequence, if you time it.

1.5 Reset

The in-game reset panel restores one subsystem, or the whole town, to golden state over the bus without a container bounce, and re-arms a fresh flag for that scope. ./reset.sh -y from the shell is the harder hammer, and also clears the scoreboard run state. Reset between cohorts, and any time the town gets into a state you did not intend.

1.6 What you should notice

Nothing here required a memory-corruption bug or a piece of malware. The whole range is misconfiguration, missing authentication, cleartext, and reused credentials. That is the point, and it is what the rest of this book is about.
