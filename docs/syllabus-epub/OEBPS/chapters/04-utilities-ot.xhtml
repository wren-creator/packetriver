Session 4: The Utilities

Goal for this session: make the physical town misbehave. Water, power, the widget-factory line, and sewage are real Modbus/TCP soft-PLCs. Traffic is commanded over an open MQTT bus. Rail is a raw-TCP console. There are no memory-corruption exploits in this session, because none of these protocols require one.

4.1 Modbus has no opinion about who you are

Modbus/TCP is from 1979. Request-response over port 502, reading and writing coils (single bits: pump run commands, keyswitches) and registers (16-bit words: setpoints, sensor values). No authentication, no session, no signing. If you can open the port, you are the HMI as far as the PLC is concerned. FrostyGoop, the malware that took the heat out of 600 buildings in Lviv in January 2024, used function code 6, write single register, the same call the HMI makes every second.

water127.0.0.1:5502stop the high-lift pump and mains pressure bleeds out over a few ticks; houses go dry, then a main bursts

Each plant's flag sits in an input-register block that only reads back while the PLC is in maintenance mode (a coil you set). The tool is pymodbus, or the bundled modbus_attack.py. Real-world parallels: Oldsmar, Florida, 2021 (the setpoint change was the attack); Maroochy Shire, Queensland, 2000 (an insider released roughly 800,000 litres of sewage via radio to pumping stations); the Ukraine grid attacks of 2015 and 2016. MITRE ATT&CK for ICS: T0855 (unauthorized command message), T0831 (manipulation of control), T0836 (modify parameter), T0813 (denial of control).

4.2 The widget factory has a second door

Alongside the process plane there is a management plane. The line-management box (factory-snmp) runs a real net-snmp agent with the shipped community strings. public reads the whole line-management subtree, and the reconciliation key is one of the OIDs. A read-write community lets you snmpset the line-enable OID and stop the line from the enterprise side. Same physical result as the Modbus path, a different plane. Default SNMP community strings on OT and network gear are a standing Shodan and pen-test finding (CWE-1392). Fix: SNMPv3 with auth and priv, no default communities, and no write access from the enterprise network.

And there is a timing puzzle: if a rail car is being loaded (the hopper gate is open) at the moment the train is routed onto the spur and derails into the factory, it is a pile-up rather than a stopped line. Throughput hard-zeros and the line jams on top of the fire.

4.3 Traffic: the bus will take an instruction from anyone

The event broker carries the signal controllers' command topic with no ACL and no auth. Publish to it and a crossroads locks all-green; the crash counter climbs a few seconds later and downtown gridlocks. A separate engineering-mode topic takes a short PIN and, on a match, publishes the flag (non-retained, so the Level 2 rotation actually takes it away). The tool is mosquitto_pub / mosquitto_sub, or traffic_attack.py. Unauthenticated MQTT brokers exposed to the internet are a standing Shodan finding; roadside signal and ITS controllers have repeatedly shipped with default or absent auth (the 2014 University of Michigan traffic-signal study). Fix: broker ACLs and auth, signed commands, and keep the control bus off any routable path.

4.4 Rail: a raw console and a shell

The loop/spur switch has a raw-TCP maintenance console with default credentials, and one of its commands hands your input straight to a shell, so it is a command-injection foothold, not a label field (CWE-78). Either one reads the maintenance key. set switch spur throws the switch; do it before the train reaches the branch, which is under the "R" of "Main Rail Line" on the map, and the train routes onto the spur, runs it down to the widget factory over about five seconds, and piles in. The factory catches fire. The Lodz tram incident of 2008 is the canonical "someone threw the switches" story. Fix: drop the raw console, or put it behind auth and a source allowlist, and never shell out on operator input.

4.5 What you should notice

Every one of these is the control channel trusting its own reachability. The PLC trusts anything on port 502. The broker trusts anything that can publish. The console trusts anything that can open the socket. The fix is almost never "patch the protocol", because you cannot. It is segmentation, a source allowlist, and moving the control plane somewhere the enterprise and the internet cannot route to.
