About the Author

Britley Hoff is the Technology Owner for Linux on IBM Z at Blue Cross Blue Shield of South Carolina: ten years of hands-on mainframe work, RACF, z/OS, TN3270, TSO, z/VM, on top of twenty-five years in Linux and general IT, including Linux on Z. GED, no college, certifications and real-world experience instead. Also an author, a maker, and a self-described Sovereign AI advocate.

Packet River is the third range in a set. Widgetorium is a deliberately vulnerable web-application lab with its own "Widgetorium 101" syllabus. Cross Creek is an ICS/OT range, a mock reverse-osmosis water plant and a power substation on real Modbus, EtherNet/IP, and S7comm, with a "Cross Creek 101" syllabus. The three share a toolchain, a safety posture, and a house style: every weakness is real, every weakness has a working hardened counter-build, and every command in the instructor edition was run against the actual containers before it went to print.

The bias in all three is the same, and it is worth stating plainly. The interesting attacks are not the clever ones. They are default credentials, a protocol with no authentication, a flat network, a system nobody wanted to touch. That is what is working right now, on water systems and substations and banks and the boxes that still run payroll. A range that teaches the boring stuff well is more useful than one that hopes a zero-day falls in your lap.
